MLA Style from the Modern Language Association is a standard for how to format your paper and cite sources. In ENGL 1181, we use MLA conventions common to the humanities.

MLA Style Formatting

  • Double-space the entire paper; use a clear 11–12 pt font (e.g., Times New Roman, Calibri, Arial).
  • Set 1″ margins on all sides.
  • Indent the first line of each paragraph by 0.5″ (use the Tab key).
  • Heading at upper-left on page 1: your name, instructor’s name, course, date. No cover page.
  • Running header (LastName space page#) in the upper-right on pages 2+.
  • Provide in-text citations for all quoted, paraphrased, and summarized material.
  • If you use endnotes, place them on a separate page before the Works Cited.
  • Include a Works Cited page; entries should match your in-text citations and use a hanging indent.

Additional Writing Guidelines

  • If an author’s gender is unknown, use the singular “they.” MLA guidance
  • Favor precise, clear wording and vocabulary you are comfortable using while developing academic voice.
  • For fuller guidance, see the MLA Handbook (9th ed.) and the MLA Online Guide.

In-Text Citations

Two common patterns:

  • Author mentioned in the sentence → add page number in parentheses if available.
    Example: Smith notes similar conventions in a related video (14).
  • Parenthetical reference → put author’s last name and page number at the end of the sentence.
    Example: The preview video includes a long caption with the release date (Jones).

Basic In-Text Citation Format

(Author’s Last Name Page#) — e.g., (Smith 14). If the author is already named in the sentence, include only the page number in parentheses.

Since you'll be referencing social media posts or comments instead of books or traditional sources, your citations will look a little different (see below).

Social Media In-Text Citations

Use the account username; for Facebook, use the account name.

If you include the name/username in the sentence, don’t repeat it in parentheses.

Examples

A video by @CrazyShook39 includes a caption with the release date and purchase links.

The caption lists the release date and retailers (@CrazyShook39).

Works Cited Page

Your Works Cited page is the last page of your paper; list entries alphabetically with a hanging indent. A typical social-media entry looks like:

Author and/or username. “Post/Caption/Title.” Platform, Date, URL.

Examples

@Tiiffxny. “Halter tops worn backwards are now my favorite thing??? What item of clothes should I style next??” YouTube Shorts, 7 Oct. 2022, https://www.youtube.com/shorts/cZ5vgfiFP84

u/douglonious. “My water currently here in central Texas.” Reddit, 27 Sept. 2024, https://www.reddit.com/r/Wellthatsucks/comments/1fqc9c0/…

Why I Started Cooking for Myself

When I moved into my first apartment, I had never cooked anything more complicated than instant ramen. The kitchen felt like another classroom, one where I didn’t know any of the answers and where every burner setting looked like a test I was about to fail. But after a semester of fast food and frozen pizzas, I realized something had to change. Learning to cook for myself has saved me money, made me healthier, and given me a kind of control over my time that I did not expect to find inside a kitchen.

The financial reasons were the first ones I noticed. According to a recent article in the Journal of College Student Development, the average undergraduate spends close to three thousand dollars a year on restaurant food and takeout (Lopez 24). When I started writing down my own spending, I was easily hitting that number without trying. Cooking even a few meals a week at home cut my grocery bill compared to what I had been spending at the campus food court, and I still got to eat the foods I actually liked.

Beyond the savings, cooking has slowly changed the way I think about food and time. Michael Pollan argues that preparing your own meals is one of the most direct ways to take charge of your health (13). I am not sure I think about it that philosophically, but I have noticed I pay closer attention to what is going into a meal when I am the one… (and so on…)

1

Heading Block

Top-left of page 1 only: your name, your professor’s name, the course, and the date — one per line. Double-spaced like the rest of the paper. Use day-month-year (no commas).

2

Running Header

Your last name and the page number sit in the top-right corner of every page — half an inch from the top. Set this once in your word processor and it repeats automatically.

3

Title

Your title goes centered, in regular text — not bold, italic, underlined, or in quotes. Capitalize the important words. Don’t add an extra blank line above or below it.

4

First-Line Indent

Each new paragraph starts with the first line indented half an inch — one Tab key press, set to 0.5”. The indent is what signals a new paragraph.

5

No Extra Blank Lines

Don’t add an extra blank line between paragraphs. The indent at the start of each new paragraph is the only marker you need. Keep the double-spaced rhythm steady all the way down.

6

Double Spacing

Every line of an MLA paper is double-spaced: heading block, title, body, and works cited. Set line spacing to 2.0 in your word processor and leave it that way.

7

Font and Size

Use a standard, readable font at 12-point — Times New Roman is the classic choice (Calibri or Arial 11-point are also common). One font for the whole paper. Don’t switch.

Works Cited page

Williams 5

Works Cited

  • @cookwithben. “Quick dorm-friendly pasta with just five ingredients.” TikTok, 22 Sept. 2024, www.tiktok.com/@cookwithben/video/123456789.
  • Garcia, Maria. “Five Pantry Staples Every College Student Needs.” Bon Appétit, 14 Mar. 2024, www.bonappetit.com/story/college-pantry-staples.
  • Lopez, Daniel. “Food Spending Among Undergraduates: A Decade of Change.” Journal of College Student Development, vol. 64, no. 3, 2023, pp. 22–39.
  • Patel, Anjali. The College Cook’s Companion. HarperCollins, 2021.
  • Pollan, Michael. Eat Less, Cook More. Penguin Press, 2022.
  • Reichl, Ruth. My Kitchen Year: 136 Recipes That Saved My Life. Random House, 2015.
1

“Works Cited” Heading

The words “Works Cited” go centered at the top — regular text, not bold, not italic, no quotation marks around them. Capitalize like a title.

2

Hanging Indent

Each entry starts at the left margin, and every line after the first is indented half an inch. Set this in your word processor’s paragraph settings so you don’t do it by hand.

3

Alphabetical Order

Entries are arranged alphabetically by the first word of each entry — usually the author’s last name. For a social media post with no real name, alphabetize by the handle (skip the @ symbol when sorting).

4

Double Spacing (Still!)

The works cited page is also double-spaced — both within each entry and between entries. Don’t add extra space between citations.

5

Italicize Major Works

Italicize book titles, journal names, magazine names, websites, and platform names (like TikTok). These are the bigger “container” works that hold smaller pieces inside them.

6

Quotation Marks for Shorter Works

Article titles, web page titles, and social media post text use quotation marks. These are the smaller pieces that live inside a larger container.
